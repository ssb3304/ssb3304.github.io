01. About

I'm an M.S. student in Biomedical Engineering at UNIST, working in the BCI Lab under Prof. Sung-Phil Kim. My research investigates visual perception paradigms in EEG-based brain–computer interfaces — designing systems that read intent directly from the brain.

What drives me is a simple fascination: how neural signals can bridge thought and action. With that lens, I aim to build technologies that

  • enhance human–machine collaboration,
  • enable communication for people with severe motor impairments, and
  • support adaptive neurorehabilitation.

I draw on cognitive neuroscience, signal processing, and machine / deep learning to push BCIs beyond the lab — toward systems that meet real human needs.

Beyond research, I'm a food enthusiast and a dedicated traveler. Hunting down authentic local cuisine and unfamiliar cultural narratives keeps my curiosity sharp — I bring the same taste for adventure to every problem I work on.

02. Research

My research lies at the intersection of neuroscience and engineering, focusing on understanding and interfacing with the human brain.

BCI / BMI

Brain-Computer & Brain-Machine Interfaces enabling direct neural communication with external devices.

Neural Encoding & Decoding

Decoding vision and motor control signals from neural activity to understand brain representations.

Cognitive Neuroscience

Investigating the neural mechanisms underlying cognition, perception, and decision-making.

Brain Imaging

Leveraging neuroimaging modalities (EEG, fMRI) to observe and analyze brain structure and function.
